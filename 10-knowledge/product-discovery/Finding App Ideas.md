# Finding App Ideas

> [!abstract] TL;DR
> App ideas come from systematic problem-hunting, not inspiration. Five reliable sources: personal friction, public complaints, niche communities, capability shifts, and clone-and-improve. Most ideas should die at the filter: frequent + painful + reachable audience + buildable v1 + willingness to pay.

## Idea sources

1. **Mine your own friction** — log for a week: repetitive annoyances, spreadsheet-as-workaround, "why isn't there an app that…", chaining 2–3 apps for one job. Built-in motivation and domain knowledge.
2. **Look where people complain** — Reddit ("is there an app that", "I wish"), 1–3★ App Store/Play Store reviews of popular apps (complaints = feature list or competitor wedge), G2/Capterra for SaaS gaps, X search for "someone please build".
3. **Scratch a community's itch** — pick a niche you understand; niches are underserved because big companies ignore them. "Generic to-do app" is dead; "shift-swap app for nurses" is a business.
4. **Ride a shift** — new AI capabilities, new regulations, new platforms/APIs. New capability = new app opportunities.
5. **Clone-and-improve** — rebuild a proven app for a different audience, geography, or platform, or with one thing done dramatically better.

## The filter (most ideas die here)

- Is the problem **frequent** and **painful**? (Vitamins get ignored; painkillers get paid for.)
- Can you **reach** the people who have it?
- Can you build a useful **v1 in reasonable scope**?
- Would someone **pay** or clearly change behavior for it?

## Strongest solo-dev patterns

- **Incumbent with paywall anger** — loud 1★ subscription complaints on a proven category = validated demand + ready differentiation.
- **Privacy as the product** — "your data never leaves the device" as a marketable feature, not a compromise.
- **Local-market gaps** — categories big Western players ignore (e.g. SEA-specific niches: kos management, motorbike maintenance, big-wedding planning).
